Thesis progress · RL for PDEs

Backward maps, exact error splitting, and Burgers

Ali Tayyar · for the meeting with Joseph Bakarji · 8 October 2026 · covers the work since the last meeting (about 24 September)

In short

  1. New formulation. The agent no longer moves walkers forward. It builds a map backward from the query point $z^*$: every point gets a stencil (5 neighbours below it), down to the initial condition and the walls. Values are then computed forward.
  2. The error at $z^*$ splits exactly into one piece per point: $e(z^*)=\sum_p\beta_p\delta_p$ (influence × local error). This is the discrete adjoint / dual-weighted-residual (DWR) identity. It lets every stencil choice get its own reward, instead of one reward at the end.
  3. Linear advection–diffusion: the method works (much better than classical 2nd-order schemes per computed point), but RL is not needed there: one fixed stencil found by brute force beats the agent.
  4. Burgers ($\alpha=0.1$): the error estimate had to be re-derived (the linear one misses the nonlinear terms). With the new estimate, choosing the stencil point by point beats every fixed stencil (0 of 18,694) and the 2nd-order schemes (3× LW's accuracy with half the points).
  5. RL on Burgers: after fixing the state and ranking the actions, the agent reaches the greedy rule (return −0.51) but does not go beyond it yet.

1Setup and definitions

The goal is the value of the PDE solution at one space-time point $z^*$, as accurately as possible while computing as few other points as possible.

PDE
$u_t + a(u)\,u_x = \alpha u_{xx}$ on $[0,1]$, $u=0$ at both walls, $u(x,0)=\sin\pi x+\tfrac12\sin 2\pi x$, $\alpha=0.1$. Linear case: $a=c=1$. Burgers: $a(u)=u$.
Grid
$n_x=100$, $\Delta x=0.0101$, $\Delta t=4.59\times10^{-4}$ (diffusive CFL, $r=\alpha\Delta t/\Delta x^2=0.45$). A point is $(i_x, i_t)$.
Query $z^*$
$(50, 20)$: the middle of the domain, 20 time levels up, unless stated otherwise.
Known data
The initial condition ($i_t=0$) and the walls ($i_x=0,99$). Free, never computed.
Stencil
The 5 neighbours $q$ used to compute a point $p$. They are chosen from a window of 21 cells: up to 3 cells to each side and 1 to 3 levels below $p$.
Offsets
$\Delta x_q=x_q-x_p$, $\Delta t_q=t_q-t_p<0$, and $\xi_q=\Delta x_q-a\,\Delta t_q$: the neighbour's offset in a frame moving with the flow at speed $a$.

The predictor (thesis §2.5, with one correction)

The value at $p$ is a weighted sum of its neighbours: $\hat u_p=\sum_q w_q\hat u_q$. Expanding each $u_q$ in a Taylor series about $p$ and replacing $u_t$ by the PDE leaves 3 conditions on the weights:

$$\sum_q w_q=1,\qquad \sum_q w_q\,\xi_q=0,\qquad \sum_q w_q\Big(\tfrac12\xi_q^2+\alpha\Delta t_q\Big)=0 .$$

5 neighbours and 3 rows: we take the minimum-norm weights. A stencil is admissible if the rows have rank 3, cond $\le10^4$ and $\|w\|_1\le 2$. With 3 points one level down the predictor reproduces Lax–Wendroff exactly (checked to 5 digits).

correctionThe third row in the thesis PDF (eqs. 2.35–2.36, §3.3.2) is written as $\sum w(\tfrac12\Delta x^2+\alpha\Delta t)$. For advection–diffusion it must use $\xi$, not $\Delta x$. All results here use the corrected row.

2The backward map

  1. Build backward. Start at $z^*$ and give it a stencil. Every neighbour that is not known data becomes a new point that also needs a stencil. Repeat until every branch ends on the IC or a wall.
  2. Order. Points wait in a queue and are expanded latest $t$ first. Since neighbours are always below, every point that uses $p$ is expanded before $p$.
  3. Forward pass. Once the map is finished, compute the values bottom-up from the known data, level by level, up to $z^*$.

The map is a directed graph with no loops (neighbours are strictly earlier), so the forward pass is a single sweep. Reusing a point that is already in the map costs nothing; a neighbour that is not yet in the map is a new point.

3Influence and the exact error identity

Influence $\beta_p$
How much an error at $p$ changes the value at $z^*$. $\beta_{z^*}=1$. When $p$ is expanded with weights $w$, each neighbour receives $\beta_q \mathrel{+}= \beta_p w_q$. Because of the latest-$t$-first order, $\beta_p$ is complete when $p$ is expanded.
Local error $\delta_p$
$\delta_p=u_p-\sum_q w_q u_q$, with exact values: how wrong $p$'s stencil is even if its neighbours were exact.

Since $\hat u_p=\sum_q w_q\hat u_q$ is linear in the neighbours, the errors $e_p=u_p-\hat u_p$ satisfy $e_p=\delta_p+\sum_q w_q e_q$. Unrolling this from $z^*$ down to the known data (where $e=0$) gives

$$e(z^*)=\sum_{p\in\text{map}}\beta_p\,\delta_p ,\qquad |e(z^*)|\le\sum_p|\beta_p\,\delta_p| \;\;(\text{the exact bound}).$$
  • verifiedThe identity holds to all printed digits (linear and Burgers).
  • It is the discrete adjoint, or dual-weighted-residual (DWR) representation of Becker & Rannacher (2001), applied to a meshfree space-time map. It is the exact version of thesis Corollary 2.7 (error accumulation), which only gave an inequality.
  • Why the bound matters: the terms $\beta_p\delta_p$ have different signs and can cancel, so the true error can be small by luck. The bound has no cancellation: it measures how good a map really is. We report both.
  • Why it matters for RL: the total error is a sum of one term per point, so every stencil choice can be graded on its own term at the moment it is made.

4The score: an error estimate without the exact solution

$\delta_p$ needs the exact solution, so it cannot be the reward. The score $s_p$ estimates $|\delta_p|$ from the stencil's geometry. The 3 rows cancel the Taylor terms up to order 2. For the linear PDE the leftover terms come in fixed combinations, the heat polynomials in $\xi$:

$$T_3=\xi^3+6\alpha\Delta t\,\xi,\qquad T_4=\xi^4+12\alpha\Delta t\,\xi^2+12(\alpha\Delta t)^2,\qquad s_p=\frac{|\sum_q w_qT_3|}{6L^3}+\frac{|\sum_q w_qT_4|}{24L^4},$$

where the unknown derivatives $u_{xxx},u_{xxxx}$ are replaced by sizes $1/L^3,1/L^4$ with $L=1/(2\pi)$ (shortest mode of the IC). workedFor the linear PDE it ranks stencils correctly: correlation 0.98–0.99 between log score and log true error, over-estimating by about 2×.

For Burgers this score fails and is replaced (section 8).

5The RL problem

Episode
Build one map for $z^*$. The current point is the latest-$t$ pending point.
Action
One window cell. 5 picks complete a stencil, then the point is expanded and the next point comes up.
Mask
A cell may be picked only if some admissible stencil contains it together with the picks so far, so a bad stencil is impossible. Points with a single admissible stencil (next to the IC) are played automatically.
Reward
Given when a stencil is complete: $r_p=-\dfrac{|\beta_p|\,s_p}{B_{\rm LW}}-\lambda\,\dfrac{\text{new points}}{N_{\rm LW}}$.
Return
The sum of rewards: $G=-\dfrac{B}{B_{\rm LW}}-\lambda\dfrac{N-1}{N_{\rm LW}}$, with $B=\sum_p|\beta_p|s_p$ (estimated bound) and $N$ the number of points. $B_{\rm LW},N_{\rm LW}$ are the same two numbers for the Lax–Wendroff map of the same $z^*$, so LW scores $\approx-1-\lambda$ and returns are comparable across queries.
$\lambda$
The price of one point. $\lambda=1$ in all runs here.
Cap
If the map passes $3N_{\rm LW}$ points the episode stops; every unexpanded point costs its worst stencil.
Training
MaskablePPO, $\gamma=1$, GAE $\lambda=1$ (each pick judged by the actual rest of the episode), 12 parallel environments, 5M steps (about 35 min on the lab machine).

No exact solution is used in the reward or the state, only for reporting error and bound.

6What came before, and did not work

  • droppedForward walkers (thesis ch. 5). They learned to reach $z^*$ but not accuracy; nothing learned at $t^*=150$ after 50M steps (constant reward in ~80% of episodes, geometric error growth).
  • droppedTerminal reward only on a backward graph: one number per map, same credit-assignment problem.
  • droppedNeighbours in all directions (same level or later): loops in the map make $(I-W)$ singular; 13% of states had error above $10^{-2}$. Hence neighbours strictly below.
  • resultSmall window (2 levels): the agent found a checkerboard pattern that turned out to be a fixed stencil (rank 3 of 240 fixed stencils; the best fixed stencil beat it).
  • failedLong horizons ($t^*=60$–$100$): PPO was worse than FD (episodes of 20–30k picks). The plain lowest-score rule, however, stays stable to $t^*=1000$ (bound $1.1\times10^{-5}$ against FD-3's $6.3\times10^{-4}$).

8Burgers: $u_t+u\,u_x=\alpha u_{xx}$, $\alpha=0.1$

Same IC, walls and grid. Exact solution by Hopf–Cole ($u=-2\alpha\phi_x/\phi$, $\phi$ a cosine series; checked: PDE residual about $10^{-7}$, IC to $3\times10^{-13}$). At $\alpha=0.1$ the solution stays smooth: the hump moves right and decays, no shock. We kept $\alpha=0.1$ because the horizons are short; with small $\alpha$ the solution would barely change before $t^*$.

8.1 Why Burgers is different for us

Locally, Burgers is advection–diffusion with speed $a=u(p)$: a different speed at every point. The best stencil depends strongly on that speed. Interior window, best stencil by score at each speed, and how much worse the stencil that is best at $a=1$ becomes:

So one stencil cannot serve all points, which is what makes a point-by-point choice worthwhile.

8.2 The speed is needed before any value exists: a coarse guess

The map is built backward, but the weights, the score and $\beta$ all need $a=u(p)$ while building. We take it from a coarse guess: Lax–Wendroff on a 5× coarser grid (20 points × 42 time levels up to $T=0.5$), then at each coarse level a sine series through the 20 values gives $u,u_x,\dots,u_{xxxx}$ exactly for the fit; linear in time onto our grid.

  • Accuracy in the cone below $z^*$: within about 1% for all five derivatives at $(50,20)$; up to 12% for $u_{xxxx}$ at $(50,100)$.
  • The guess only chooses stencils and weights. The values are computed from the neighbours' computed values, so a poor guess gives worse choices, never wrong numbers.
  • Cost: only the coarse levels up to $t^*$ count for a query. At $(50,20)$ that is 1 level × 18 points = 18 points (the full guess, 840 points, serves every query up to $T$).
  • This is the usual practice in goal-oriented adaptivity: linearise around an approximate (coarse) primal solution.

8.3 The linear score fails for Burgers

didn't workEven with the exact speed at each point, the linear score correlates only 0.71 with the true local error. The lowest-score map built with it was 5× worse than Lax–Wendroff (554 points, error $9.5\times10^{-5}$, bound $2.5\times10^{-4}$). The cause is not the speed estimate: with the exact speed, the lowest-score map was still no better than LW ($4.0\times10^{-5}$).

Reason: the predictor and the score freeze one speed $a=u(p)$ for the whole stencil. In Burgers the speed is the solution itself, so it changes across the stencil. Expanding Burgers properly (Cauchy–Kowalevski: every time derivative replaced through the PDE) gives three extra terms that a frozen speed misses:

Taylor termCoefficient with a frozen speedExtra term in Burgers
$\Delta x\,\Delta t$$\alpha u_{xxx}-u\,u_{xx}$$-u_x^2$
$\tfrac12\Delta x^2\Delta t$$\alpha u_{xxxx}-u\,u_{xxx}$$-3u_xu_{xx}$
$\tfrac12\Delta t^2$$\alpha^2u_{xxxx}-2\alpha u\,u_{xxx}+u^2u_{xx}$$2u_x(u\,u_x-2\alpha u_{xx})$

They grow with how far the stencil reaches in time, so the linear score favoured deep stencils that are in fact bad. Adding only the size of these terms, or cancelling $\sum w\,\Delta x\Delta t$ with a 4th row, did not help (correlation 0.75 / 0.78): the extra terms and the linear terms have signs that partly cancel.

8.4 The new score

workedThe new score is the stencil's error on the local Taylor polynomial of the solution, with all Burgers terms and the actual derivatives (signs included) from the guess:

$$s_p=\Big|\sum_q w_q\,P_p(\Delta x_q,\Delta t_q)\Big|,\quad P_p=u_x\Delta x+u_t\Delta t+\tfrac12u_{xx}\Delta x^2+u_{xt}\Delta x\Delta t+\tfrac12u_{tt}\Delta t^2+\tfrac16u_{xxx}\Delta x^3+\tfrac12u_{xxt}\Delta x^2\Delta t+\tfrac1{24}u_{xxxx}\Delta x^4,$$

kept up to weight 4 (a $\Delta t$ counts like two $\Delta x$, since $\alpha\Delta t\sim\Delta x^2$). With exact derivatives it matches the true local error to about 2% (correlation 1.00; weight 3 only: 0.85).

10Open questions and next steps

Where we stand

  • Holds: the exact error identity, the backward map, and (for Burgers) the derived Taylor score with a coarse guess. Choosing stencils point by point with this score beats fixed stencils and 2nd-order schemes.
  • Not shown yet: that RL does better than the greedy rule. At this query it learned greedy exactly.

Next (no training needed first)

  1. Look-ahead test: at each point, try the few best stencils, finish the map greedily for each, keep the best. If it barely beats greedy, there is little to learn at this query; if it clearly does, the agent has room to improve.
  2. Larger $t^*$ ($(50,50)$, $(50,100)$) with the rules first: the speed varies more, and choices have longer consequences. Training there costs hours (maps 6–18× larger).
  3. Min-score weights for Burgers: spend the 2 free weight degrees on the Burgers leftover; about 10× in the linear case.
  4. $\lambda$ sweep (greedy first, it is cheap): curves instead of single points on the accuracy plot.
  5. Multi-query on Burgers, where the guess is shared by all queries.
  6. Thesis PDF updates: corrected third row, backward map and identity, both scores.
